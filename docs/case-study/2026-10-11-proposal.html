The products, the prices, the warehouse in Cheyenne and your WooCommerce back office all stay. What changes is how a visitor feels in the first ten seconds, how much the site does for them after that, and how much of each visit turns into a sale, a sign-up or a referral.

The short version

Five things to know before you scroll

  1. Nothing you rely on changes. WooCommerce, your products, prices, orders, Afterpay and Klarna all stay exactly where they are. Only the shop window is rebuilt, so there is no risk to the business while it happens.
  2. Your customer sees themselves in the first three seconds. The new site opens on real faces, women and men in their thirties and early forties, different backgrounds and skin, each with a concern and a ritual. The current site opens on a logo and a search box. People buy from a brand that looks like them.
  3. Faster pages mean fewer lost sales. The home page downloads about a fifth of the data of the current one. Most of your customers browse on a phone at night, and a page that hangs for a few seconds is a customer who leaves. Google rewards speed too.
  4. It brings people back and gets them talking. A shelf that remembers each customer, guides that answer what they search for, reviews on every product, a members' circle and a monthly letter. Those are the pieces that turn one order into a regular, and a regular into a referral.
  5. It is already built. Everything in this document is clickable today, not a drawing. That means the timeline is short, the cost is known, and you can judge the real thing rather than a promise.
